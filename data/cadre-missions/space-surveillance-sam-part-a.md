# Introduction

## Purpose of this document

CADRE Part A provides a concise, standardized synopsis of the mission or
project to support costing and analysis activities. Its primary purpose
is to consolidate information from multiple technical and programmatic
source documents into a single, normalized reference.

Specifically, CADRE Part A:

- Summarizes key mission characteristics in a consistent and structured
  format

- Aggregates information drawn from multiple authoritative technical
  documents

- Reduces the need for analysts to repeatedly consult disparate source
  materials

- Ensures a common baseline description is used across analyses

- Improves efficiency, traceability, and consistency in downstream
  costing work

In practical terms, CADRE Part A functions as a time-saving reference
document that enables analysts to quickly understand the mission context
without re-interpreting or reconciling multiple source documents.

## Purpose AND SCOPE of MISSION

Space Adherence Mission (SAM) is a Canadian Earth-observation mission
intended to provide persistent, repeatable space-based information
supporting Canadian situational awareness, surveillance, and monitoring
in the Arctic and northern maritime approaches. The mission is
government-led, owned and operated by the Canadian Space Agency (CSA),
in collaboration with the Department of National Defence (DND).

The mission responds to the increasing strategic, transportation,
environmental, and commercial significance of the Canadian Arctic.
Declining seasonal sea ice and increasing activity in northern waterways
increase the value of persistent observations of vessels, sea-ice
conditions, infrastructure, transportation corridors, and changes in the
coastal and terrestrial environment.

SAM is intended to provide an independent Canadian space-based sensing
capability that complements existing terrestrial, airborne, maritime,
allied, and commercial information sources. The two-spacecraft
architecture is intended to provide coordinated and near-continuous
observation of designated Arctic areas of interest while maintaining
Canadian control of mission operations and mission data.

The project is conceptually inspired by Canada\'s SAPPHIRE military
satellite program, particularly its government-industry delivery model,
dedicated operational ground segment, mission-assurance approach, and
long-duration government operations. SAPPHIRE is treated as a
programmatic heritage reference rather than a direct technical or cost
analogue because SAM is an Earth-observation mission with a different
payload, orbit, ground segment, and operational concept.

# General Descriptive Information

## Mission Overview

The Space Adherence Mission (SAM) is a proposed two-spacecraft
Earth-observation mission designed to provide persistent, repeatable
observations of Canada\'s Arctic and northern maritime approaches.

SAM is intended to complement existing Canadian surveillance and
information systems by providing observations over geographically
extensive and sparsely populated regions where conventional terrestrial
surveillance infrastructure is limited.

The two SAM spacecraft will operate as a coordinated constellation in a
highly elliptical, high-inclination orbit selected to maximize dwell
time over the Canadian Arctic. The preliminary reference orbit uses a
low-altitude portion near 550 km with an inclination of at least 85
degrees; final apogee, orbital period, phasing, and observation geometry
remain subject to mission analysis.

The mission\'s broad observation objectives include:

- Arctic maritime activity;

- sea-ice distribution and seasonal changes;

- northern transportation corridors;

- selected infrastructure;

- changes in coastal and terrestrial environments; and

- other government-defined areas of interest.

The system consists of two substantially similar spacecraft,
Earth-observation payloads, a commercial launch service, government and
contractor-provided ground infrastructure, mission-control and planning
systems, data-processing and archive capability, 24-hour government
mission operations, and associated project-management, assurance, and
sustainment activities.

## Context

The conceptual mission draws organizational and programmatic inspiration
from SAPPHIRE, Canada\'s space-based surveillance satellite developed
for DND. SAPPHIRE demonstrates relevant Canadian heritage in military
space operations, spacecraft development, government-industry
cooperation, satellite ground operations, mission assurance, and
long-duration spacecraft operations.

SAPPHIRE demonstrates relevant Canadian heritage in:

- military space operations;

- spacecraft development;

- government-industry cooperation;

- satellite ground operations;

- mission assurance; and

- long-duration spacecraft operations.

SAPPHIRE\'s mission and sensor architecture were substantially different
from the proposed SAM Earth-observation mission. It is therefore used
only as a programmatic heritage reference; the SAM cost and schedule
baseline is driven by the SAM architecture, current PM schedule,
procurement model, and finalized CADRe Part C / Part D assumptions.

Reference industry responses and market observations are retained only
as concept-development evidence; they do not establish SAM procurement
partners. The finalized procurement baseline is defined in CADRe Part D
and identifies CSA, COS, NorthStar Imaging Technologies, Earth Daily
Analytics, Northern Launch Services, and Canadian university/research
institutions as the relevant organizations.

The SAM CADRe baseline in this document is the controlling Part A
reference for the current cost estimate. Any change to the mission
architecture, schedule, procurement model, or organizational baseline
shall be reconciled with the finalized CADRe Part C and Part D
documents.

## High-Level Mission Architecture 

Owned and operated by CSA, in collaboration with the Department of
National Defence, the mission consists of:

- A Government Segment: CSA project management, systems engineering,
  payload authority, mission planning, mission operations, governance,
  security, and technical oversight, with DND as the principal defence
  stakeholder.

- A Space Segment: two substantially similar spacecraft, each with a
  common bus and a VNIR/SWIR Earth-observation payload, operated as a
  coordinated constellation in the selected HEO.

- A Launch Segment: a commercial launch service procured for the
  two-spacecraft constellation; Northern Launch Services is the current
  launch-service organization in the SAM procurement baseline.

- A Ground Segment: contractor-provided and integrated infrastructure
  for mission planning, spacecraft control, receiving stations, data
  processing, calibration, quality control, archiving, and secure
  dissemination. Earth Daily Analytics provides and integrates the
  required ground-segment elements.

- An Ops Segment: Government of Canada personnel performing 24-hour
  mission operations, including monitoring, command execution, anomaly
  response, orbit and attitude monitoring, mission planning, and
  coordination of the two spacecraft.

- A Science Segment: Canadian scientific leadership, university/research
  teams, and supporting infrastructure for calibration and validation,
  data analysis, scientific data products, user support, and scientific
  exploitation of SAM observations. No international science partner is
  assumed in the baseline.

# Space Segment Description

The space segment consists of a constellation of 2 spacecrafts, each
with a bus and a payload.

## Spacecraft Bus 

The SAM spacecraft bus will provide the structural, electrical, thermal,
computational, communications, attitude-control, and propulsion
functions required to operate the Earth-observation payload in the
selected HEO. The bus design shall support a common architecture across
both spacecraft and the mission-level requirements for storage,
autonomy, pointing, communications, lifetime, and end-of-life disposal.

Two substantially similar spacecraft will be developed to reduce
non-recurring engineering, manufacturing complexity, and operations
cost. The spacecraft will share a common bus architecture while allowing
payload-specific equipment and software to be accommodated as required.
The same qualified design will be used for Flight Model 1 and Flight
Model 2, with each flight unit undergoing its own acceptance
verification.

Key functions of the bus include:

- Structural subsystem

- Electrical power generation and distribution

- Command and Data Handling (C&DH)

- Thermal subsystem

- Attitude Determination and Control System (ADCS)

- Propulsion subsystem

- Telemetry, Tracking and Command (TT&C)

Reference design note: the detailed component examples that follow were
adapted from an EDA/DEFIANT small-spacecraft reference and are used only
as notional heritage for first-order costing. They are not SAM flight
allocations. The current SAM baseline is controlled by the quantitative
mission requirements, COS bus procurement, and Part C / Part D
baselines.

### Command an Data handling

The Command and Data Handling (C&DH) subsystem will provide the central
computing and data-management functions of each spacecraft.

The C&DH subsystem will:

- Receive and execute commands;

- Collect telemetry;

- Control payload interfaces;

- Manage mission data;

- Control onboard storage;

- Execute fault-management functions;

- Monitor spacecraft health;

- Support autonomous operation;

- Manage spacecraft configuration;

- Maintain mission timing; and

- Interface with the TT&C subsystem.

Each spacecraft shall provide at least 1 TB of usable onboard
mission-data storage.

The C&DH subsystem shall support at least 72 hours of autonomous
spacecraft operation without ground intervention. With a two-spacecraft
constellation, the minimum total onboard storage is therefore 2 TB
across the mission. The planning case of 125 GB/day of downlinked
mission data per spacecraft corresponds to roughly 8 days of storage at
full-rate-equivalent volume before accounting for compression,
prioritization, and additional operational constraints.

**Fault Management**

The C&DH architecture will provide fault detection and recovery
functions.

Potential functions include:

- Watchdog monitoring;

- Health monitoring;

- Fault detection;

- Fault isolation;

- Fault recovery;

- Safe-mode entry;

- Redundant-equipment selection; and

- Recovery following loss of ground communications.

**Data Management**

The C&DH subsystem will receive mission data from the payload and store
the data before transmission to the ground segment.

The design will consider:

- Payload data-generation rate;

- Observation duration;

- Number of observations;

- Onboard storage;

- Communications opportunities;

- Data compression;

- Data prioritization;

- Data latency; and

- Ground processing capacity.

Because the two spacecraft will operate as a coordinated constellation,
configuration and software management shall ensure that both spacecraft
remain compatible with the common mission architecture, software
baseline, time reference, operational procedures, and mission-planning
interfaces.

### Thermal Subsystem

The Thermal Control Subsystem will maintain spacecraft and payload
components within their qualified temperature limits.

The thermal environment will vary throughout the highly elliptical
orbit.

Thermal analysis will therefore consider:

- Solar radiation;

- Earth infrared radiation;

- Albedo;

- Eclipse;

- Orbital position;

- Spacecraft attitude;

- Payload heat generation;

- Electronics dissipation;

- Communications equipment;

- Battery temperature; and

- Propulsion components.

The thermal architecture will use passive thermal-control techniques
wherever practical.

Potential components include:

- Multi-layer insulation;

- Thermal coatings;

- Radiators;

- Thermal straps;

- Heat pipes;

- Heaters;

- Thermostats; and

- Temperature sensors.

**Thermal Analysis**

The spacecraft will undergo hot-case and cold-case orbital thermal
analyses.

The analysis will establish:

- Component temperature ranges;

- Heater requirements;

- Radiator sizing;

- Thermal gradients;

- Payload thermal stability;

- Battery thermal limits; and

- Propulsion thermal constraints.

**Cost Drivers**

Thermal-system cost will be affected by:

- Payload heat generation;

- Spacecraft power;

- Radiator area;

- Heater requirements;

- Thermal stability requirements;

- Propulsion configuration;

- Thermal analysis complexity; and

- Environmental testing.

### Attitude Determination and Control Subsystem

Attitude Determination & Control

- Coarse attitude determination will use sun sensors, a magnetometer,
  and rate sensing; fine attitude determination will use star trackers.

- Three-axis attitude control will use reaction wheels with
  magnetorquers for detumbling and momentum management. The architecture
  shall support the mission-pointing requirements during imaging and
  high-rate communications.

- Absolute pointing accuracy target: ≤0.05° during imaging operations.

- The spacecraft will use mission-pointing profiles appropriate to the
  HEO observation geometry rather than assuming permanent nadir pointing
  in every spacecraft mode.

- 3 vacuum core magnetorquers will be utilized.

- Orbit determination will use the onboard navigation solution. A
  preliminary real-time position-knowledge target of \<10 m is retained
  from the reference design and is subject to validation through the
  final flight-dynamics and navigation analysis.

Orbit Determination

- Orbit determination will be done by obtaining position estimates from
  the onboard GPS receiver.

- Real-time orbit position knowledge of \<10 m.

- GPS receiver will also provide time reference for the satellite.

## Payload

Source: EDA reference payload material

Each payload consists of:

- A VNIR imager

- A SWIR imager

# Launch Segment Description

The preliminary spacecraft mass allocation implies a combined launch
mass of ≤1,200 kg for the two spacecraft, with a target combined
spacecraft mass of approximately 1,000 kg before any launch
adapter/dispenser allocation. Final launch-vehicle sizing shall use the
verified flight mass properties and applicable launch margins.

The two SAM spacecraft will be launched using a single commercial launch
service into the selected HEO configuration. Northern Launch Services is
the current launch-service organization in the procurement baseline;
final launch-vehicle selection remains subject to launch-interface and
orbit-insertion analysis.

The launch estimate shall treat launch insurance as a separate cost
element because the reference launch price excludes insurance.

At 250 GB/day of combined downlinked data, five years of continuous

## Safety and Mission Assurance

Initial assessment: Mission Class B. The program requires formal mission
assurance, qualification, acceptance, verification, configuration
control, and readiness reviews. Safety and mission-assurance effort is a
direct cost and schedule driver because both flight spacecraft must
independently demonstrate conformity with the qualified design before
launch.

## Qualification and Acceptance Strategy, MODEL Philosophy

The SAM qualification and acceptance strategy will verify that both
spacecraft are capable of surviving launch and performing their required
mission functions throughout the planned operational lifetime. The
approach uses one Qualification Model (QM) followed by two flight models
(FM-1 and FM-2), with engineering-model hardware used only where
necessary to verify interfaces and reduce development risk.

The Qualification Model will represent the flight configuration as
closely as practical and will undergo spacecraft-level environmental and
functional testing, including vibration, thermal-vacuum, electromagnetic
compatibility, and end-to-end functional testing. Payload interfaces,
communications, C&DH, power, attitude control, propulsion, mission-data
handling, and ground interfaces will be verified. Existing qualification
evidence may be used only where applicable to the SAM configuration and
operating environment.

Following successful qualification, FM-1 and FM-2 will be manufactured
using the qualified design. Each flight spacecraft will undergo its own
functional, electrical, communications, payload, software, and
appropriate environmental acceptance testing. FM-2 is not qualified
solely by FM-1 testing; both spacecraft must independently demonstrate
conformity with the qualified design before launch.

The qualification and acceptance program also accounts for the logistics
of operating two spacecraft. The spacecraft will be integrated and
tested individually, transported to the launch site under controlled
configuration and environmental conditions, and supported through
launch-site inspection, final functional checks, propulsion servicing if
applicable, launch readiness activities, and secure transfer of
configuration data.

Following launch, each spacecraft will undergo an individual
commissioning period targeted to complete within 90 days of launch.
Commissioning will verify deployment, power generation, attitude
control, communications, payload functionality, calibration, initial
imaging performance, flight-software configuration, and ground-segment
interfaces before the spacecraft are transitioned into coordinated
nominal operations.

All qualification and acceptance results will be documented through the
SAM verification and configuration-management process. Any anomalies
identified during testing, transportation, launch-site processing, or
commissioning will be assessed and resolved before the affected
spacecraft is declared operational. Final approval for launch and entry
into operational service will be based on completion of required
verification activities and successful Flight Readiness and Operations
Readiness Reviews.
